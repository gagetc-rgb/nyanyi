The land

Thirty-three are. Thirty-three metres.

At the road you stand level with the treetops. Then the land falls away: a steep wooded bank, gentler terraces below it, and at the very bottom a river running over stones. Build high and live in the light above the canopy, or walk down to the water, where the road disappears and only the river speaks.

Two plots share the slope, each with the road above and the river below: 18 are to the north, 15 to the south. The line between them is indicative and is set with the owner.

Questions

What buyers ask first.

Yes, as a leaseholder. The freehold title stays with the Indonesian owner, as the law requires, and you hold a notarial lease. You see the full lease terms before any deposit.

The lease runs to 2055, and an extension of up to 30 years is guaranteed in it, at the market rate for the land alone: the house you build does not raise the price. As is standard in Bali, permanent buildings stay with the land when a lease finally ends.

The lease continues. It binds the owner’s heirs and successors, and the deed bars the owner from selling or mortgaging the land while the lease runs.

The title states 3,300 m²; the 2024 survey measures about 3,810 m² within the boundary the owner showed. The title’s own measurement sheet of 2001 already draws the land from the road to the river. You pay for the 33 are, the rest comes with the land, and a re-measurement at the land office can bring the title up to date.

The zoning letter carries no flood-hazard note for this land, and the road side stands about 33 m above the riverbed. How close to the water you can build is set by the river authority with the building permit.

Living here yourself and long-term letting are straightforward. Nightly letting depends on a business licence, not on zoning: the zoning letter permits villa use, and the river-side zone names eco-tourism among its uses.
